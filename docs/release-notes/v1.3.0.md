1.3.0: heart rate after a session in Lap analysis (FBBD-225, UI-107 A, UI-108 B, UI-109), OBD-II adapters added as equipment and named on the OBD-II page (FBBD-121, UI-105 C, UI-106 A), car data that survives a dropped adapter and a stop mid-request (FBBD-269, FBBD-271), and OBD-II and heart rate on the track screen's status strip (FBBD-268, UI-110 B, UI-111 B, UI-112 A).

What changes for the driver:
- Lap analysis › Summary has a Heart rate card under the lap times: the session's average, highest and lowest heart rate, the strap's name and how many minutes of heart rate it holds.
- Lap analysis › Laps has a column with each lap's average and highest heart rate (bpm avg · max).
- Only what was recorded is shown: a session without heart rate has no card and no column, and a lap without heart rate has an empty cell, never 0 bpm. The lap comparison has no heart-rate chart.
- OBD-II adapters are equipment now (UI-105 C), and only the adapters listed in Settings › Devices read the car; the app no longer looks for one on its own. The Wi-Fi adapter the app used until now is listed automatically on update ("Wi-Fi OBD-II adapter", 192.168.0.10 : 35000), so Wi-Fi recording carries on. To add a Bluetooth adapter such as the Vgate vLinker MC+: pair it in Android's Bluetooth settings, then Settings › Devices › Add equipment lists it under "Paired Bluetooth devices" (a Bluetooth LE one also shows under "Found nearby"). Remove an adapter to stop reading the car with it.
- The OBD-II page and the Car data row in Settings › Devices name the adapter (UI-106 A): "vLinker MC+ · Bluetooth", "vLinker MC+ · ELM327 v2.3 · ISO 15765-4 CAN · 12.4 V" when connected, "vLinker MC+ not answering · trying again" while it does not answer. With no adapter listed the page says "No adapter added".
- A RaceBox whose connection stalls before data (no MTU answer, Android drops it with status 147) is now reached by changing how the next attempt connects: Android's cached services are cleared, then the MTU request is skipped, then Android's background connection is tried (FBBD-272). Each connection step is logged with its timing.
- Stopping a session while the OBD-II adapter was answering a request could crash the recorder process (it restarted at once, and the session was saved; FBBD-269). Stopping now ends car data quietly, over Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and Bluetooth LE.
- When the phone's Wi-Fi or Bluetooth comes back during a session, the adapter is tried again at once instead of up to 20 s later (FBBD-271). While it runs, a recording also tells Android that it uses a Wi-Fi network without internet, as the adapter's is. The app log says when a Wi-Fi network is lost or available, when Bluetooth goes on or off, and, every minute, that the search for an adapter is still running.
- OBD-II is read much faster on CAN cars (FBBD-270): up to six values go in one request and the adapter is told how many answers to wait for, instead of waiting out its timeout each time. On the 20 Oct Corolla log's timing this takes RPM, speed and throttle from 0.4 Hz to about 11 Hz with an adapter that honours the count (about 1.9 Hz with one that does not), and temperatures from 0.08 Hz to 2 Hz. An adapter or car that does not support this is read one value at a time, as before. The log now also lists the latest value of every channel each minute, oil temperature included.
- The track screen's status strip shows a car icon with "Connected" while the car answers over OBD-II, and a heart icon with the current heart rate while the strap sends one (FBBD-268, UI-110 B, UI-111 B, UI-112 A). Each icon shows only while its data arrives: no icon while an adapter or strap is searched for, connecting or lost, without skin contact, or when the last heart rate is more than 5 s old.
- Timing, video and coach are unchanged from 1.2.0.
