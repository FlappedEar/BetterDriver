1.0.1: the app records OBD-II car data from a Wi-Fi ELM327 adapter (FBBD-15, first step).

What changes for the driver:
- Plug the Wi-Fi ELM327 adapter into the car, join its Wi-Fi network on the phone (usually "WiFi_OBDII"; if Android says the network has no internet, choose to stay connected), then start a recording as usual. Nothing else to set up and no new screens.
- While the recording runs, the app connects to the adapter at the Wi-Fi network's gateway and at 192.168.0.10, port 35000, asks the car which standard PIDs it has, and records each one it answers: RPM, speed, throttle, pedal, engine load, coolant and intake temperatures, manifold pressure, MAF, lambda, fuel level, battery voltage and the other standard mode 01 channels. They are stored as channels named obd_<name> (for example obd_rpm) and appear in the CSV and VBO exports.
- If the adapter drops out or the engine is switched off, the rest of the recording carries on; the app reconnects on its own.
- Connects, drops, the adapter and protocol, the car's PIDs and the poll rate of every channel once a minute go to the app log (tag "Obd") and to the session events.
- GPS, lap timing, the coach and video are unchanged. Without an OBD-II adapter on Wi-Fi nothing changes.
